# Spatial Localization, Channel Attribution & Explainability for EEG Seizure Detection: A Research Survey for Phase B

## TL;DR
- **The CHB-MIT public release contains only seizure onset/offset TIMES, not per-channel or seizure-onset-zone (SOZ) ground truth** — so no paper can compute a true localization accuracy on CHB-MIT; papers either (a) redefine the task (focal vs. generalized, or lateralization/lobe), (b) validate on *other* datasets that do have labels (TUH/TUSZ, iEEG, Bern-Barcelona), or (c) validate indirectly via within-subject/within-seizure consistency, anomaly-score/null contrast, and expert visual plausibility.
- **The two most transferable, label-free attribution strategies for your wPLI+AEC adjacency + PELT pipeline are: (1) per-node reconstruction error / anomaly score from your existing GAE (no retraining), and (2) node-strength / degree / edge-weight change (Δ-connectivity) computed directly across the detected change point** — both are unsupervised, require no SOZ labels, and are validated in the literature by within-subject consistency, top-k electrode overlap, and contrast against a surrogate/null distribution rather than against absent CHB-MIT SOZ labels.
- **The closest peer-reviewed comparators** are Grattarola et al. (ESWA 2022, attention-GNN, iEEG, AP@K vs. clinician SOZ), Tang et al. (ICLR 2022, self-supervised DCRNN, TUSZ, occlusion-based localization/coverage), Ho & Armanfard EEG-CGS (AAAI 2023, self-supervised anomalous-channel detection on CHB-MIT + TUSZ, the only prior method requiring NO seizure data in training), and DeepSOZ (MICCAI 2023, supervised attention pooling, TUH). Reviewers' most common criticism — "attention ≠ explanation" — must be pre-empted with a faithfulness check (edge perturbation/deletion).

## Key Findings

1. **CHB-MIT lacks SOZ / per-channel labels — confirmed.** The BIDS CHB-MIT release (Zenodo 10259996) comprises 23 pediatric subjects, 664 `.edf` recordings (129 containing seizures), 198 seizures total, sampled at 256 Hz/16-bit, using a "double banana bipolar montage with 18 channels"; seizures are referenced only as start/stop times. There is no per-channel "seizure channel" or seizure-onset-zone annotation in the public files. Studies that report "localization accuracy" on CHB-MIT obtain their ground truth from *outside* the dataset — e.g., Chung et al. (Front. Neurol. 2024) had neurologists *re-annotate* seizure locations, and the "explainable hybrid DNN" (Biomed. Signal Process. Control 2024) validated lobe/hemisphere against externally sourced clinical labels. This is the single most important fact for Phase B: you cannot compute a true SOZ-overlap metric on CHB-MIT, so your validation must be a proxy.

2. **When SOZ labels are absent, the field uses four proxy-validation strategies:** (a) *Problem redefinition* — reduce to focal-vs-generalized or lateralization/lobe classification, which CHB-MIT partially supports at the case level; (b) *Within-subject / within-seizure consistency* — show the same channels/edges recur across a patient's seizures (used by DeepSOZ's intra-seizure/intra-patient consistency analysis); (c) *Expert visual inspection / plausibility* — show attributed channels fall in clinically plausible regions (temporal-lobe dominance); (d) *Anomaly-score contrast / null* — show attributed channels have significantly higher anomaly score than the rest, or than a surrogate distribution (EEG-CGS, EEG-GCA).

3. **Methods most compatible with your existing 18×18 wPLI+AEC adjacency + GAE + change points (no retraining, no labels):** per-node reconstruction error from the GAE; node-strength/degree/eigencentrality change across the change point; edge-weight delta (top-k edges that change most); and edge-perturbation/occlusion faithfulness testing. These map directly onto your pipeline and are all unsupervised.

## Details

### 1. CORE — Spatial localization / channel attribution / explainability on scalp-EEG connectivity

**Grattarola, Livi, Alippi, Wennberg, Valiante — "Seizure localisation with attention-based graph neural networks."** *Expert Systems With Applications* 203:117330, 2022 (peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1016/j.eswa.2022.117330; bioRxiv preprint 2020.12.03.409979 also exists and is explicitly not peer-certified). Dataset: clinical **iEEG**, 8 human subjects (6 with a clinically defined SOZ, 2 without) plus two brain simulators. **Ground truth: YES** — clinician-identified SOZ electrodes. Attribution method: functional networks (Pearson correlation and phase-locking value), an edge-aware message-passing GNN with an **attention-based readout**; attention scores summed in a window around onset give per-electrode importance, ranked. **Validation: Average Precision at K (AP@K) / MAP@K (K=2,5,10)** of the attention ranking vs. clinician SOZ, at electrode and strip scales. Key numbers: AP@5 > 0 for every patient (≥1 SOZ electrode in top-5 every time); "the GNN achieves a perfect AP@2 score (average rankings) in six out of eight cases when using PLV, indicating a high chance of localising at least two relevant electrodes per patient." Detection (weak-supervision task, reported separately): Corr. GNN ROC-AUC 79.56 ± 10.82, PR-AUC 81.24 ± 10.37, versus a baseline of 75.93 / 80.62. Note the useful finding that **correlation graphs gave better detection while PLV gave better localization** — relevant to your wPLI-vs-AEC choice. Crucially, localization "does not require any prior information regarding the SOZ" — the model is trained only on onset/offset weak labels, exactly your regime.

**Tang, Dunnmon, Saab, et al. — "Self-Supervised Graph Neural Networks for Improved Electroencephalographic Seizure Analysis."** ICLR 2022 (peer-reviewed conference; arXiv 2104.08336). Dataset: **TUH/TUSZ** v1.5.2 (5,499 EEGs, 19 channels). **Ground truth: YES** — TUSZ provides channel×time annotations; they define M^annot ∈ ℝ^{N×T} with 1 where a channel shows seizure at second j. Model: DCRNN (diffusion-convolutional recurrent GNN) with distance-graph and correlation-graph variants + self-supervised pre-training. Attribution: **occlusion/saliency** — zero-fill one channel-second at a time, measure logit change, normalize to [0,1] → saliency map M. Two metrics: **coverage** (recall-like) and **localization** (precision-like); "precise" = localization > 0.8. Result: "pre-trained Corr-DCRNN precisely localizes 25.4% focal seizures... Dense-CNN only localizes 3.5% focal seizures precisely (localization score >0.8)... providing 21.9 points improvement over a prior state-of-the-art CNN" (Dist-DCRNN 21.8%; non-pretrained variants 6.3%) — i.e., a **21.9 percentage-point** gain, not a relative one. Detection AUROC 0.875; classification weighted-F1 0.749. This is the cleanest template for an *occlusion-based, precision/recall-against-channel-labels* validation — but it requires channel labels you won't have on CHB-MIT.

**Ho & Armanfard — EEG-CGS, "Self-Supervised Learning for Anomalous Channel Detection in EEG Graphs: Application to Seizure Analysis."** AAAI 2023, 37(7):7866–7874 (peer-reviewed; DOI 10.1609/aaai.v37i7.25952; arXiv 2208.07448). Datasets: **CHB-MIT and TUSZ v1.5.2**. Method: brain graph with electrodes as nodes; contrastive self-supervised learning sampling positive/negative sub-graphs by radius; anomaly score per channel from local structure+feature reconstruction. **This is the single most important comparator for you: per the abstract, "we propose to detect seizure channels and clips in a self-supervised manner where no access to the seizure data is needed"** — fully unsupervised, patient-independent-capable, and it explicitly attributes anomaly to *channels*. Validation: anomaly-score-based channel ranking, establishing state-of-the-art and outperforming supervised baselines. For CHB-MIT, channel-level "ground truth" is not available, so its channel-detection evaluation leans on the focal/generalized distinction and anomaly-score contrast.

**Tian & Zhang — EEG-GCA, "EEG-based epilepsy detection with graph correlation analysis."** *Frontiers in Medicine* 2025 (peer-reviewed; DOI 10.3389/fmed.2025.1549491). Uses inter-channel correlation graphs + a weight-sharing GNN + KL-divergence regularization; detects abnormal channels/segments by measuring correlation between two views at test time. Like EEG-CGS, **trained without any seizure data**. Validation of channel attribution: for generalized seizures they show all abnormal channels light up; for focal seizures the high-anomaly channels concentrate on specific regions "that match the ground-truth" — i.e., expert-plausibility / focal-vs-generalized contrast rather than a quantitative SOZ-overlap metric.

**"Explainable hybrid DNN" (Biomed. Signal Process. Control, 2024, S1746809424015428 / S174680942400380X).** CHB-MIT, Siena, Helsinki (neonatal). Attribution: **SHAP Gradient Explainer** giving event-wise and patient-wise explanations pinpointing brain lobe and hemisphere. Reports **localization/lateralization accuracy of 85.43% (CHB-MIT), 86% (Siena), 79.4% (Helsinki)** — but note these are validated against externally-sourced clinical lobe/hemisphere labels, not against per-channel labels in the CHB-MIT release, and the granularity is lobe/hemisphere, not electrode. Treat these numbers as lateralization plausibility, not SOZ-electrode accuracy.

**DeepSOZ (Shama, Jing, Venkataraman), MICCAI 2023** and the earlier **SZTrack (PMC8884583)**. Scalp EEG, TUH (120 patients) / Johns Hopkins (34 patients, 201 seizures). **Supervised.** Transformer encoder → global branch + LSTM (temporal detection) and channel-wise branch → attention-weighted multi-instance pooling (spatial SOZ). Trained only on onset/offset weak labels ("our dataset does not contain fine-grained channel-level information"), yet outputs per-channel SOZ predictions validated against clinical SOZ annotations where available, plus **intra-seizure and intra-patient consistency** as a trustworthiness proxy. The **BUNDL** extension (arXiv 2410.19815) adds Bayesian uncertainty-aware training (KL loss + MC-dropout) and reports +3.2 pts seizure-level and +2.9 pts patient-level SOZ accuracy over cross-entropy on TUH. DeepSOZ's consistency-based validation is directly borrowable for CHB-MIT.

**Channel-annotated deep learning (Biomed. Signal Process. Control 2024, S1746809424015428).** TUH (channel-annotated), plus tested on CHB-MIT and a private RMH set. Uses **DeepSHAP** to attribute predictions to channels; sensitivity of channel attribution vs. ground-truth annotation = **0.59 overall** (0.63 on focal FNSZ/ABSZ, 0.57 on GNSZ). This honest, modest number is useful to cite as a realistic ceiling for scalp channel attribution and to temper reviewer expectations.

### 3. CROSS-REFERENCE — How SOZ-labeled datasets validate localization (borrowable proxies)

- **Grattarola (iEEG):** AP@K / MAP@K of an electrode ranking vs. clinician SOZ, at multiple K and multiple spatial scales (electrode and strip). **Borrowable idea:** rank your channels by Δ-node-strength or reconstruction error and report top-k *consistency* rather than accuracy when labels are absent.
- **Tang (TUSZ):** occlusion-based coverage (recall) and localization (precision) against channel×time labels; threshold on normalized saliency. **Borrowable idea:** the precision/recall framing, but substitute a *within-patient reference* (channels consistently flagged across that patient's seizures) for the missing labels.
- **Dual-Task GNN on sEEG (arXiv 2505.23669, 2025, preprint):** node-level SOZ accuracy 94.72% with clinician-annotated sEEG (20 pediatric patients, 51 seizures), thresholded Pearson-correlation graphs, LOOCV — shows the value of leave-one-patient-out for patient-independence.
- **GCN epilepsy-propagation-network (Biomed. Signal Process. Control 2022, S1746809422000118):** unsupervised GCN + DBSCAN on HFO propagation to generate SOZ *pseudo-labels* — a template for label-free localization validated by proximity to the HFO-network center.
- **Bern-Barcelona (focal vs. non-focal iEEG):** the dataset *is* the ground truth by construction (focal vs. non-focal channel pairs from 5 temporal-lobe-epilepsy patients); typical validation is focal/non-focal classification accuracy (reported 94–99% across studies). **Borrowable idea:** frame your CHB-MIT attribution as a *lateralization / focal-vs-generalized* proxy, which is checkable at the case level.
- **Gamma-band AEC in SOZ (Sci. Rep. 2024, s41598-024-67120-8):** gamma amplitude-envelope correlations and node strength/eigencentrality are **elevated within the SOZ** in focal epilepsy; wPLI eigencentrality is elevated in the fast-ripple band within SOZ. **This directly validates your gamma-AEC ensemble choice and motivates using node strength/eigencentrality change as the attribution statistic** — there is a physiological prior that the SOZ shows elevated gamma-AEC node strength.

### 4. SYNTHESIS for the thesis

**(a) Common approaches to spatial attribution on connectivity-based EEG models:** (i) attention weights (Grattarola, DeepSOZ, GAT models); (ii) gradient/occlusion saliency (Tang DCRNN, SHAP/DeepSHAP hybrids); (iii) per-node/channel anomaly or reconstruction score (EEG-CGS, EEG-GCA, autoencoders); (iv) graph-theoretic node metrics (strength, degree, eigencentrality, clustering) and their change over time.

**(b) How the field handles the no-SOZ-label problem on CHB-MIT:** it (1) redefines to focal/generalized or lobe/hemisphere lateralization; (2) imports external clinician labels or re-annotation (Chung 2024, hybrid-DNN 2024); (3) validates on labeled datasets (TUSZ, iEEG, Bern-Barcelona) and only *demonstrates* on CHB-MIT; or (4) uses within-subject consistency and anomaly-score contrast as proxy validation. Honest papers explicitly state CHB-MIT has no channel labels (Tang note; DeepSOZ note; REST note that "CHB-MIT does not include seizure types").

**(c) Methods compatible with your 18×18 wPLI+AEC adjacency + GAE + PELT, with NO retraining/labels:**
1. **Per-node GAE reconstruction error** at/around the change point — you already compute reconstruction error globally for the anomaly score; decompose it per node. This is the single most natural, zero-cost attribution.
2. **Δ node-strength / degree / eigencentrality** of the wPLI and gamma-AEC graphs across the PELT change point — rank channels by the magnitude of connectivity change. Physiologically grounded (gamma-AEC node strength ↑ in SOZ).
3. **Δ edge-weight (top-k changing edges)** — since you already threshold top-20% edges, report which edges enter/leave or change most at the change point.
4. **Edge/node perturbation (occlusion)** — delete a node/edge, recompute the anomaly score, measure the drop; this doubles as a *faithfulness* check that pre-empts the "attention≠explanation" criticism.

**(d) Pitfalls and reviewer criticisms to pre-empt:**
- **"Attention is not explanation"** (Jain & Wallace 2019; Wiegreffe & Pinter 2019; "Is Attention Interpretation?" arXiv 2207.13018, 2022) — attention weights may not be faithful. Mitigation: use a perturbation-based faithfulness test, not attention alone.
- **Volume conduction / bipolar-montage artifacts** — apparent connectivity between adjacent electrodes can be spurious. wPLI mitigates zero-lag/volume-conduction (that is its purpose); state this. AEC should use leakage/orthogonalization correction where possible.
- **No ground truth on CHB-MIT** — never claim "SOZ accuracy" on CHB-MIT; claim consistency / plausibility / lateralization instead.
- **Patient-specific vs patient-independent inflation** and **window vs event scoring** — keep your SzCORE event-level numbers separate from window-level literature numbers.
- **Redundancy of graph metrics** — node strength, eigencentrality, and clustering are often mutually redundant (Sci. Rep. 2024); report one primary and note the correlation.
- **Generalized seizures have no focal SOZ** — attribution is only meaningful for focal cases; segment CHB-MIT cases accordingly.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (immediate, no new training):** Add per-node GAE reconstruction error and Δ-node-strength (wPLI + gamma-AEC) at each PELT change point. Rank the 18 channels; report the top-k. This is the lowest-risk, fully-unsupervised Phase-B deliverable.

**Stage 2 (validation without labels):** For each patient with ≥2 seizures, compute **within-patient consistency** of the top-k attributed channels across seizures (Jaccard overlap / rank correlation), mirroring DeepSOZ's intra-patient consistency. Compute a **surrogate/null** by shuffling window order or phase-randomizing signals and show the observed attribution concentration exceeds null (permutation p-value). Report **lateralization plausibility**: does the attributed hemisphere match the CHB-MIT case's documented dominant side where available?

**Stage 3 (faithfulness):** Run **edge/node occlusion** — remove the top attributed node/edge, recompute the ensemble anomaly score, and show a significant drop vs. removing a random node. This directly answers the "attention≠explanation" reviewer.

**Stage 4 (external quantitative check, optional but strong):** Port your attribution method to a labeled dataset — **TUSZ** (channel×time labels; use Tang's coverage/localization metric) or **Siena** — to report one honest quantitative localization number, then argue transfer to CHB-MIT.

**Benchmarks that change the plan:** If within-patient top-k consistency (Jaccard) is < ~0.3 or not above null, do NOT claim localization — report it as a negative/exploratory result. If occlusion of top nodes does not drop the anomaly score more than random, your attribution is unfaithful and should not be published as explanation.

## Caveats
- Several strong sources are **preprints, not yet peer-reviewed**: SzCORE-challenge report (arXiv 2505.18191), BUNDL (arXiv 2410.19815), Dual-Task sEEG GNN (arXiv 2505.23669), SpaTeoGL (arXiv 2602.11801). SzCORE itself IS peer-reviewed (Dan et al., *Epilepsia* 2024, DOI 10.1111/epi.18113), which defines the event-based scoring (sensitivity, precision, F1, FP/24h) your thesis adopts.
- The hybrid-DNN "85.43% CHB-MIT localization" number is lobe/hemisphere-level against externally sourced labels, not electrode-level against the CHB-MIT release — do not present it as SOZ-electrode accuracy.
- Exact per-patient AP@K tables in Grattarola and the exact CHB-MIT AUROC for Li et al. (2022, Sci. Rep. PMC9643358) could not be fully extracted from tables and should be verified against the PDFs before citing precise values.
- CHB-MIT seizure/channel counts vary slightly across papers (182 vs 198 seizures; 18 vs 22 vs 23 channels) due to montage inconsistencies and excluded files (e.g., chb12's non-standard montage, chb24 exclusions) — state precisely which subset and montage you use.
